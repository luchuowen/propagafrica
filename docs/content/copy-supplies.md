Eyebrow `STAGE 01 · PREPARE`
H1 **Growing media, trays and tools.**
Standfirst
What a young plant sits in decides how evenly it roots. A medium that holds a little too much water, or a tray filled a few millimetres short, shows up three weeks later as a batch that finishes unevenly — long after anyone can do anything about it.

Caption `FIG. 03 · CELL SECTION, 128-CELL TRAY · SCALE 1:1.6 · REV. A`

# /supplies/root · SHEET 05 OF 14

Eyebrow `STAGE 03 · ROOT`
H1 **Misting, benching, irrigation and monitoring.**
Standfirst
A freshly grafted plant cannot drink yet, so the air around its leaves has to stay wet without the base of the cell ever sitting in water. Holding both at once, for three weeks, is the hardest thing a propagation house does.

Title block
```
DRAWING    PA-005
SUBJECT    PROPAGATION ENVIRONMENT
BASIS      SPECIFIED AND SOURCED
CONTROL    VAPOUR PRESSURE DEFICIT
SHEET      05 OF 14
```

**Figure PA-005** — mist bench in elevation: bench, three trays, mist line with three nozzles and spray cones, nozzle pitch dimensioned 1.1 m in `--signal`, a sensor drawn at canopy height with a leader, and a dashed control loop from sensor back to the valve.
Caption `FIG. 05 · MIST BENCH, ELEVATION · SCALE 1:20 · REV. A`

Figure note
The sensor belongs at canopy height, not at the gable. The two read differently, and the plant lives at the canopy.

### What we specify and source

Equipment at this stage is designed around your house and sourced to that design. It is not held in stock.

- Misting and fogging — nozzle selection, line layout, pump and filtration
- Climate control — heating, cooling, ventilation, humidity domes and covers
- Benching and racking — fixed, rolling, and heated benches
- Irrigation — drip, ebb and flow, capillary matting
- Shade and screening — internal and external, by light transmission
- LED growing lights and control
- Automation and control panels

### Table — Propagation monitoring

| Measurement | Why it is measured | Logging interval |
|---|---|---|
| Air temperature and humidity | The two inputs to vapour pressure deficit | 1 min |
| Vapour pressure deficit | The variable the misting decision is actually made on | 1 min |
| Substrate moisture | Waterlogging at the base of the cell | 5 min |
| Substrate conductivity | Salt build-up under repeated misting | 5 min |
| Light, PAR | The load driving water loss from the leaf | 1 min |

Notes block
Moisture sensors are calibrated to the substrate actually in use — coco peat, a vermiculite blend and a peat formulation each read differently, and a sensor calibrated for one is wrong in another. Supplied as hardware, dashboard and support together, with threshold alerts and export of the full history.

Eyebrow `STAGE 04 · PROTECT`
H1 **Nursery hygiene.**
Standfirst
Hygiene in a propagation house is not a cleaning routine. It is a written protocol with a strength, a contact time and a record — and for anyone exporting roses to Europe, it is audited.

Title block
```
DRAWING    PA-006
SUBJECT    NURSERY HYGIENE
STRENGTH   10 % · 12 % AVAILABLE CHLORINE
PACK       20 L · 200 L
SHEET      06 OF 14
```

**Figure PA-006** — dilution schematic: concentrate drum, a measuring cylinder showing 83 ml, a plus sign, a 10 L water vessel, an arrow, and the resulting 1 000 ppm working solution. The arithmetic printed above in `--signal`.
Caption `FIG. 06 · DILUTION SCHEMATIC, TOOL DIP · REV. A`

Figure note
The arithmetic is printed because the failure mode is a supervisor guessing a capful. A dip made at a tenth of the intended strength is not biosecurity, it is the appearance of it.

### Table — PRO-SAN working dilutions from a 12 % concentrate

| Application | Target | Concentrate per 10 L | Contact time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool dip, between plants | 1 000 ppm | 83 ml | 30 s or more |
| Footbath, refreshed daily | 1 000 ppm | 83 ml | on entry |
| Bench and surface wash | 500 ppm | 42 ml | 5 min or more |
| Irrigation line shock | 200 ppm | 17 ml | 60 min or more |
| Empty-house terminal clean | 2 000 ppm | 167 ml | 10 min or more |

Working: 12 % available chlorine is 120 g per litre. 83 ml carries 10 g. Ten grams in 10 litres is 1 g/L, which is 1 000 ppm.

Notes block
Sodium hypochlorite, CAS 7681-52-9. HDPE packaging, compliant for road transport. Shelf life 90 days from manufacture at 25 °C or below, stored out of direct light — hypochlorite loses strength with heat and time, so every drum is dated and stock is rotated rather than held. Safety data sheet and certificate of analysis supplied with every consignment.

Second note
Farms exporting roses to the European Union work under a systems approach that became mandatory on 26 April 2025 under Regulation (EU) 2024/2004. Nurseries certified to GLOBALG.A.P. Integrated Farm Assurance for Plant Propagation Material are assessed against control points covering hygiene procedure, cleaning of reusable materials, disinfection of equipment between batches, and the batch number of the substrate used. Those are the requirements this range is bought against.
